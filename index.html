About

I'm a final-year student in Genetic Engineering and Biotechnology, working mostly at the computational end of biology — genome mining, structural modelling, and molecular docking — but grounded in wet-lab fundamentals. My research so far has focused on finding antibacterial candidates from unconventional sources and building fully in-silico pipelines that a resource-limited lab can actually run.

Outside my own research, I founded BioPC — A Bioinformatics Lab of Research and Training, built around a conviction I keep coming back to: the biggest barrier to computational biology in Bangladesh isn't talent, it's access — to training, mentorship, and tools. I'm trying to close that gap.

Research

ongoing — thesis FLA / ESKAPE

Mining Bacteriolytic Genes from Free-Living Amoeba (FLA) Genomes: A Computational Framework for Targeting ESKAPE Pathogens

An entirely in-silico pipeline that mines free-living amoeba genomes for bacteriolytic genes with activity against ESKAPE pathogens — the group of bacteria responsible for most drug-resistant hospital infections. The pipeline combines literature curation, genome mining, gene library construction, and structural docking (AlphaFold2 + AutoDock Vina), with a built-in exclusion filter for proteins shown to enhance rather than inhibit bacterial virulence.
